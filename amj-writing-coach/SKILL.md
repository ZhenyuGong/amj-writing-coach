# AMJ writing coach

Use the seven editorials as a decision framework, not as a guarantee of acceptance or a substitute for current journal instructions. Read [references/series-guide.md](references/series-guide.md) for the section or problem at hand. Use [references/sources.md](references/sources.md) when attributing guidance or checking a DOI.

For a broader AMJ topic, consult the [From the Editors collection inventory](references/fte-collection.md) to locate relevant editorials. This inventory is bibliographic; do not imply that its articles have all been fully read or distilled into the guidance below.

## Work with a manuscript

1. Identify the user's research question, evidence, target outlet, study type, and present draft stage. Infer what is available; ask only for material genuinely needed to continue.
2. Locate the paper's central contribution in one or two concrete sentences. Separate the phenomenon, theoretical claim, empirical evidence, and boundary of inference. Do not invent findings, methods, or citations.
3. Diagnose the highest-impact weakness using the relevant part(s) of the series. Explain the evidence from the draft and the consequence for the reader. Distinguish the editorial's advice from your own editorial judgment.
4. Revise the actual text or produce a section plan, depending on the request. Keep claims aligned with the supplied design and results. Preserve the author's voice and disciplinary terminology where useful.
5. Give a short change log and any unresolved evidence requests. When the user supplies a manuscript, point to passages or section headings rather than issuing generic advice.

## Routing

- Topic and contribution: Part 1.
- Research question, sample, measures, identification, or design: Part 2.
- Opening paragraphs and contribution framing: Part 3.
- Theory and hypothesis logic: Part 4.
- Methods, analyses, and results reporting: Part 5.
- Discussion, theoretical implications, and limitations: Part 6.
- Inductive qualitative studies: Part 7, plus whichever earlier parts fit the paper.

Apply only the relevant parts. For a whole-manuscript review, trace the chain from question to design, theoretical argument, evidence, and implications; rank the revisions by their effect on that chain.

## Source discipline

These editorials were published in 2011–2012. Check the current AMJ author guidelines for submission requirements and avoid presenting historical advice as a current rule. If an answer relies on a specific editorial, cite its DOI from the source index. Paraphrase instead of reproducing extended passages. The locally downloaded PDFs are for the user's private research library; they are not part of this distributable skill.
