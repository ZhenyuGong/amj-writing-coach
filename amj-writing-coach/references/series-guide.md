# Seven-part writing guide

Use these as diagnostic questions and revision moves. They synthesize the editorials; they are not quotations or formal AMJ acceptance criteria. For bibliographic details and DOI links, see [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. Topic choice — Colquitt & George (2011)

Ask why the phenomenon matters, what is genuinely surprising, who might learn from it, and whether the paper can make a focused contribution. Test significance, novelty, curiosity, scope, and practical actionability. If the topic is broad, name the smallest consequential question the available evidence can answer.

## 2. Research design — Bono & McNamara (2011)

Match design to the claim. Inspect construct measurement, sampling, timing, levels of analysis, model choice, and rival explanations. If the data are cross-sectional or observational, calibrate causal or process claims to what the design supports. Propose a design repair or a narrower claim, with its trade-off.

## 3. Setting the hook — Grant & Pollock (2011)

An introduction should make a reader understand why the question matters, what current work leaves unsettled, and what this study changes. Identify the opening phenomenon, the precise tension or puzzle, the research question, the approach, and the contribution. A missing-citation “gap” alone is weak motivation; build the consequence of resolving it.

## 4. Grounding hypotheses — Sparrowe & Mayer (2011)

For each hypothesis, identify its theoretical premise, causal or interpretive mechanism, expected direction, and scope condition. Check that the propositions form one coherent argument and that the hypothesis is neither merely obvious nor unsupported by the preceding reasoning. Keep citations tied to the actual premise they support.

## 5. Crafting methods and results — Zhang & Shaw (2012)

Audit completeness, clarity, and credibility. In Methods, report enough about sample, procedure, measures, and analysis for a reader to evaluate the evidence. In Results, align analyses with hypotheses, make the order easy to follow, and report material findings transparently, including findings that complicate the story. Do not imply analyses were prespecified when the record does not establish that.

## 6. Discussing implications — Geletkanycz & Tepper (2012)

Return to the question promised in the introduction. Explain what the results change in the theoretical account, where that change applies, and what remains uncertain. Avoid a second Results section, a list of disconnected implications, or claims extending past the data. Treat limitations as specific boundaries that help interpret the contribution.

## 7. Qualitative research — Bansal & Corley (2012)

For an inductive paper, show how evidence and theorizing developed together. Make the data-to-concept path visible through clear methods, selected evidence, and an intelligible story. Use the flexibility of qualitative inquiry without presenting the process as mechanically predetermined. Evaluate qualitative work on the strength of its insight and evidentiary trace, not on whether it mimics a quantitative hypothesis test.

## Whole-paper check

Write a five-line spine: (1) consequential phenomenon, (2) unresolved question, (3) conceptual answer, (4) evidence and design, (5) implications and boundary. If two lines do not connect, repair that link before line editing.
